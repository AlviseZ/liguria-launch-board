How to read it

Flyable: wind in sector, within limits Marginal: cross, light or near the limits No: wrong direction No: too strong, rain or lee side
  • Each cell is one daylight hour. The arrow shows where the wind blows to, the number is mean wind in km/h.
  • The compass next to each name shows the launch sector: solid green is good, amber is marginal, red is a direction to avoid.
  • A wind direction counts as in sector within 22.5° of a listed direction, and marginal up to 40°.
  • Model check: the main verdict uses the Open-Meteo best-match forecast. The chips under each strip show what ICON-2I, ICON-D2, AROME, ICON-EU and ECMWF say for the same launch and day. The 2 km models only reach two or three days ahead. When they disagree, trust the verdict less.
  • Measured: the latest reading from the nearest ARPAL anemometer within 15 km, coloured against the launch sector. A station in a harbour or on another ridge can read very differently from the launch.
  • Week overview: each cell is one day, with the mean daylight wind in km/h, the rain total over the 24 hours and the longest flyable window, plus how many of the other models call the day flyable. Tap a day to open its hourly board.

Your limits

Before you drive up

  • This is a model forecast for wind at 10 m above ground on a grid of a few kilometres. It does not see local sea breeze, rotor, thermals or cloud base. The windsock and the local pilots decide.
  • Launches that land on a beach are closed from 1 May to 30 September. The board marks them closed on those dates.
  • Launch positions come from published sources where one exists, checked against terrain height. Four use a nearby landmark and two are estimates: each launch says which it is.
  • Wind sectors come from pilot websites of different ages. Where they disagree the board takes the cautious reading and says so in the site notes. Landing agreements and access rules change: check with the local club.
  • Live stations: ARPAL OMIRL network, Bergeggi (Holfuy), Finale Ligure (Holfuy).
